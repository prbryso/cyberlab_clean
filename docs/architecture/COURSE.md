**Status:** Living Document

# Vision

Artificial Intelligence is transforming engineering.

Much of today's discussion focuses on AI tools, prompt engineering, productivity gains, and code generation.

This course takes a different approach.

The objective is not to teach engineers how to use AI.

The objective is to teach engineers how to collaborate with AI while designing, developing, documenting, and maintaining complex systems.

This course is based upon the real-world development of Systems Studio, a software platform that evolved through sustained collaboration between an experienced systems engineer and Artificial Intelligence.

Rather than presenting theory alone, the course uses Systems Studio as a continuous case study demonstrating how engineering changes when AI becomes an active collaborator.

---

# Why This Course Exists

Artificial Intelligence can now generate code, documentation, diagrams, analyses, and architectural suggestions at remarkable speed.

What AI cannot replace is engineering judgment.

As AI assumes more responsibility for implementation, the engineer's role changes.

This course explores that new role.

The purpose is not to teach engineers how to ask better questions.

The purpose is to teach engineers how to become better engineers because AI exists.

---

# Target Audience

This course is intended for:

- Software Engineers
- Systems Engineers
- Engineering Managers
- Technical Leaders
- Software Architects
- University Students
- Engineering Professors
- Product Developers
- Anyone interested in engineering with AI rather than simply using AI.

---

# Learning Objectives

By the end of this course students should understand:

- The difference between using AI and collaborating with AI.
- Prompt Engineering versus Collaborative Engineering.
- How to preserve architectural integrity.
- How to maintain context throughout long engineering projects.
- How to evaluate AI recommendations.
- When to trust AI.
- When to challenge AI.
- How AI changes the engineering process.
- Why systems thinking becomes even more valuable.
- How engineers develop judgment while collaborating with AI.

---

# Core Philosophy

The engineer remains responsible.

AI becomes a collaborator.

Judgment remains human.

Architecture remains human.

Implementation becomes collaborative.

---

# Guiding Questions

Every engineering decision should answer three questions.

## Why does this exist?

Every class, document, feature, architecture decision, lesson, or prompt should have a clear purpose.

---

## What problem does it solve?

This question was one I frequently asked engineers throughout my career.

"So what?"

If a proposal cannot clearly explain the problem it solves, it probably should not exist.

This simple question forces clarity before implementation.

---

## Who cares?

Engineering should create value.

Every proposal should identify who benefits from the solution.

Understanding the stakeholders often reveals whether the work is truly important.

These three questions apply equally to:

- Software
- Architecture
- Documentation
- Educational Content
- AI Prompts
- Engineering Decisions

---

# Working Principles

The following principles are expected to evolve throughout the development of Systems Studio.

Current principles include:

- Context is an engineering asset.
- Teach the System, Not Just the Rule.
- Preserve architecture before implementation.
- Register, don't hardcode.
- Separate knowledge from presentation.
- AI accelerates engineering but does not replace engineering judgment.
- Collaborative engineering is iterative.
- Architecture lives in the human.
- AI contributes ideas; engineers make decisions.

# Capstone Project

Unlike traditional AI courses that conclude by asking students to build an application using AI, this course concludes with a different objective.

Students will design, develop, document, and present a substantial engineering project demonstrating collaborative engineering with AI.

The capstone should demonstrate:

- Architectural thinking
- Context management
- Human-AI collaboration
- Engineering judgment
- Documentation
- Trade-off analysis
- Reflection

The goal is not simply to produce working software.

The goal is to demonstrate mature engineering in the presence of Artificial Intelligence.

This document captures the evolving vision for a future course on Collaborative Engineering with AI.

The ultimate objective is to teach engineers how to think, design, and lead in a world where Artificial Intelligence has become a capable engineering collaborator.
